Our 5-stage discovery and estimating path

A price you can build on.

Every project starts with different levels of information. Our process meets you where you are and moves you, stage by stage, toward a buildable plan and an executable price.

Illustration. Every project narrows differently. The example is the one on our process page: a 600 sq ft ADU with one and a half bathrooms, a kitchenette, a bedroom and an office.

  1. Stage 1

    Initial scope and budget range

    A quick phone call or in-person meeting to understand the project at a high level and talk through a starting budget range based on project type.

    Your investment
    No cost
    Goal
    See whether the project and budget are aligned enough to move forward.
  2. Stage 2

    General scope and math-based budget

    We define the project in general but meaningful terms, then apply math to project type, size, complexity and known scope components.

    Goal
    Move from a broad conversation to a realistic preliminary budget.
  3. Stage 3

    Feasibility and cost analysis

    This is where preconstruction becomes real. We visit the site with your future project manager and focus on rough construction, where the biggest cost and feasibility issues live: site prep, demolition, foundation, framing, rough MEP, the building envelope, insulation and drywall.

    Goal
    Strong cost confidence in the major components, and a budget suitable for permitting.
  4. Stage 4

    Preconstruction and bid building

    A partner architect builds out the plans, or we prepare straightforward submittal sets in-house. While permits are underway, we gather subcontractor pricing, materials lists, finish selections and a timeline.

    Goal
    A near-final budget based on real bids, real materials and real planning.
  5. Stage 5

    Executable proposal

    Once the project is permitted, we compare the stamped plans against our Stage 4 assumptions and make final adjustments. On many projects those changes are small.

    Goal
    Move directly into construction with confidence.

From Keith's desk

Line #26: Supervision.

Clients sometimes ask why our proposals show supervision on its own line. We could bury it inside every trade package and the total would barely change. We show it because separating it is more transparent, not less.

It pays for the work you don't see on site: client and architect coordination, problem solving when conditions surprise us, project software and documentation, safety, insurance and leadership backing up your project manager. We aim for roughly a 20% margin, and that margin runs a professional company; it isn't a jackpot.

"If something in a contract is unclear, sloppy, or confusing — ask about it. You should."Keith Berkley, owner
